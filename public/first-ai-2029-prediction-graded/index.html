My first AI 2029 prediction came due. Here is the grade.

On 18 October I published AI 2029, a scenario with 18 dated predictions and a promise to grade each one when it comes due. The first was due this month: "Democrats win the House; AI super PAC results are mixed." The US midterms were on 3 November. Grade: hit, with one word wrong.

What I predicted

The table line said Democrats win the House and the AI industry's super PACs get mixed results. The story text went further: Democrats "narrowly" take the House, electricity bills near new data centers are "the attack ad that works", and voters "dislike AI but rarely vote on it".

What happened

  • The House: Democrats won control of the House and the Senate (CNBC, 4 Nov).
  • AI money: AI super PACs spent $79 million this cycle (Fortune, 4 Nov). Their biggest bet on a competitive general-election race, about $4.8 million for Darline Graham in the South Carolina Senate race, lost 46.7% to 51.1%. Some backed candidates won, like Pete Ricketts in Nebraska. Mixed, as predicted.
  • Data centers: Graham's opponent, Annie Andrews, called her "Data Center Darline" and won (Fortune). That is the attack line I expected to work.

Where I was wrong

"Narrowly." It was not narrow. CNBC called it an epic night for Democrats, and they took the Senate too, which my scenario did not say. I under-called the size of the wave. A forecast that gets the direction right and the size wrong should say so.

"Voters rarely vote on AI" also looks shakier than I wrote it: at least one Senate race turned on a data-center line. One race is not a trend, so I leave that one open.

What comes next

Three more predictions fall due in December 2026: a frontier lab IPO that closes its first day above $1.5 trillion, an AI neocloud that restructures its debt while 2027 hyperscaler capex guidance still rises, and the S&P 500 ending 2026 up 5 to 15%. I will grade them here, with sources, in early January. The full table is on the AI 2029 page, now at 1 of 18 graded.
