The elevator did not need a better motor. It needed a stop button.

The driverless elevator was built around 1900. People walked in, saw no operator, and walked right back out. It took a strike, a recorded voice and a big red button to change that. I think AI agents in small business are standing at the same door.

The true story

Early elevators were scary. Operators stopped the car at each floor by hand and opened the doors by hand. Sometimes they made mistakes. So the industry added doors with safety bumpers and automatic stopping. Then it built a car with no operator at all. NPR calls it "the Google car of its era. And people hated it."

The historian Lee Gray told NPR: "People walked in and looked and walked right back out. They would quickly step back out and try to find someone to say where's the operator?"

The machine worked. People did not trust it. So it sat.

What changed

NPR: "in 1945, elevator operators in New York went on strike. New York City ground to a halt." The strike "prevented one and a half million office workers from getting to work." Building owners demanded a change.

The industry did not build a smarter elevator. It built a calmer one. Ads showed "children pushing the buttons and grandma riding in the elevator car." A recorded voice said: "This is an automatic elevator. Please press the button for the floor you desire."

And the voice pointed you to what NPR calls "the biggest calming device ever invented, a big red button that said, stop."

It worked. But NPR adds the hard part: "it took more than 50 years before the public became comfortable and automatic elevators became ubiquitous."

What it predicts for small business

I build AI agents for small businesses. Capability is not the thing holding them back. An agent can already write the follow-up, chase the quote and answer the late-night question.

The owner is the rider looking for the operator. They want to know: what will it send in my name? Can I stop it? Who do I call if it goes wrong?

So the race is not for a smarter agent. It is for the voice and the stop button. Clear notes on what the agent is about to do. A pause you can see. A human you can reach. Dooza Crew agents ask the owner to approve before they send or pay. That is the "stop button" of today.

I should be fair about the limits. Elevators are one machine doing one job. Agents do many jobs, and some will fail in ways a stop button cannot catch. The elevator also needed a crisis, the strike, to force the change. I do not know what the AI version of that strike is. Maybe a hiring crunch. Maybe nothing, and adoption is slow.
